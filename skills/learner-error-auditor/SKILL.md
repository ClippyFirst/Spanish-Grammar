# Learner Error Auditor

## Principle
A structural difference between Ukrainian and Spanish suggests a possible transfer challenge; it does not prove that learners commonly make a particular error.

## Procedure
1. Extract every claim such as "Ukrainian speakers usually confuse..." or "students often say...".
2. Search for empirical learner-language research, learner corpora or documented teaching evidence relevant to the learner population and proficiency level.
3. Separate documented error, plausible transfer hypothesis, individual anecdote and generic pedagogical warning.
4. If evidence is absent, rewrite as a conditional possibility ("може бути складно...") or remove the frequency claim.
5. Design a diagnostic minimal pair or task that tests the target distinction without embedding the answer in the prompt.
6. Check the item for multiple defensible answers and regional variation.
7. Explain feedback in terms of meaning and structure, not shame or deficit framing.

## Output
Claim, evidence status, target structure, likely learner level if evidenced, diagnostic item, answer rationale and caveats.

## Guardrails
Never invent error percentages, learner studies or representative anecdotes. Do not assume every Ukrainian speaker shares the same first-language experience. Do not call a transfer hypothesis a fact. Do not infer proficiency from one mistake.
