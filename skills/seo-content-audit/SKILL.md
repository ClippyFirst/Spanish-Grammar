# SEO Content Audit

## Questions
- What specific grammar question would bring a learner to this page?
- Does the opening answer that question directly?
- Are terms and examples precise enough to satisfy the intent?
- Does the page add value beyond generic summaries or search snippets?
- Are important limitations, exceptions and regional variants clearly marked?
- Are claims attributable to suitable sources?
- Are headings descriptive and navigable?
- Are neighboring pages complementary rather than competing duplicates?
- Does the page provide a useful next step through meaningful internal links?

## Procedure
Summarize likely intent in one sentence; map sections to that intent; flag missing answers, repetition and unsupported claims; compare related pages; recommend edits that improve user value. Send grammar claims to the fact-checker and citation auditor rather than using SEO intuition as linguistic evidence.

## Output
Intent statement, strengths, gaps, duplication/cannibalization risks, prioritized changes and verification criteria.

## Guardrails
Do not use keyword density as a quality metric. Do not fabricate search-volume figures or rankings. Do not remove necessary linguistic nuance to shorten text. Do not recommend AI-generated volume pages or false expertise signals.
