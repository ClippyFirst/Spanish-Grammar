# Spanish CEFR & Scope Auditor

## Purpose
Keep proficiency-level labels useful without presenting editorial intuition as official CEFR certification.

## Procedure
1. Identify every A1–C2 label or claim such as "beginner-friendly", "B2 grammar" or "required at C1".
2. Determine whether it refers to receptive recognition, explicit grammatical knowledge, controlled production or spontaneous use.
3. Consult the CEFR Companion Volume and credible curriculum/exam specifications where relevant; record the edition and exact descriptor or mapping.
4. Distinguish an official descriptor from a local teaching decision or a common textbook sequence.
5. Check prerequisites, terminology load, example vocabulary and task demands separately.
6. Recommend a level only with rationale and scope. If evidence is weak, use "suggested prerequisite" or leave unlabeled.
7. Do not assume that a grammar construction belongs to exactly one level across all curricula.

## Output
Claimed level; task type; source and exact descriptor; mapping rationale; conflicting evidence; recommended label; caveat.

## Guardrails
Do not claim official CEFR approval. Do not invent exam requirements or level assignments. CEFR communicative descriptors do not automatically specify the exact point at which every Spanish grammar topic must be taught.

## Source lead
Council of Europe, CEFR Companion Volume: https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages
