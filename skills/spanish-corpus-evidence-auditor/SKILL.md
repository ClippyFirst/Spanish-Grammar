# Spanish Corpus Evidence Auditor

## Suitable questions

Use this skill for claims about how often a form occurs, which regions use it, collocations, register, change over time or differences between written and spoken language. Do not use corpus counts alone to decide whether a form is normatively accepted.

## Source selection

Evaluate the resource against the exact research question rather than assuming one corpus answers everything. Candidate sources include:

- **Corpus ASALE**: investigate its current version, documentation, sampling design, genres, geographic coverage, query interface and access terms before use.
- **CORPES XXI**: consider for contemporary pan-Hispanic evidence; record the release/version and its documented coverage.
- **CREA**: consider its time span and sampling limits; do not present it as equivalent to a current web or spoken-language corpus.
- **Other academic or specialist corpora**: use when the target genre, region or spoken interaction is not adequately represented by the above.

Official entry points:
- https://www.asale.org/corpus-asale/
- https://www.rae.es/banco-de-datos/corpes-xxi
- https://www.rae.es/banco-de-datos/crea

Do not assume a named corpus is accessible through an API, downloadable, or queryable by the current agent. Confirm actual access.

## Workflow

1. Turn the claim into a measurable question with construction, sense, period, genre and target region.
2. Select a corpus whose documented coverage can answer that question. Explain why it is suitable and what it cannot establish.
3. Record the corpus version/release, access date, interface or tool used, query string, filters and sampling procedure.
4. Design balanced queries and compare like with like: same sense, construction, genre, period and regional sample where possible.
5. Inspect concordance lines manually. Remove homographs, irrelevant senses, quotations, duplicated documents, boilerplate and search artifacts.
6. Record raw counts and denominators where available. Normalize only when the denominator and method are defensible; do not compare counts across corpora as if sampling designs were identical.
7. Separate attestation, relative frequency, geographic distribution, register, acceptability and normative recommendation.
8. Look for counterexamples and alternative analyses. If evidence is sparse or the query is not reproducible, narrow the wording.
9. Provide a reproducible query protocol and safe wording that does not overstate the findings.

## Output

Research question; corpus/version and coverage; actual access method; query/filter details; inspected examples; counts and denominators where available; sample limitations; counterevidence; evidence strength; safe wording; reproducibility notes.

## Guardrails

Never invent corpus counts, query results, API access or imply you inspected data you did not access. A form's presence does not establish that it is common, pan-Hispanic or appropriate in every register. Absence from a search is not proof of impossibility. Corpus evidence complements, but does not replace, authoritative grammatical analysis for normative claims.
