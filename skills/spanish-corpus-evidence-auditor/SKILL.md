# Spanish Corpus Evidence Auditor

## Suitable questions
Use this skill for claims about how often a form occurs, which regions use it, collocations, register, change over time or differences between written and spoken language. Do not use corpus counts alone to decide whether a form is normatively accepted.

## Workflow
1. Turn the claim into a measurable question with construction, sense, period, genre and target region.
2. Choose an appropriate corpus and document its coverage and limitations. Candidate resources include RAE/ASALE corpora such as CORPES XXI and CREA, and other research corpora when access and licensing permit.
3. Design balanced queries and compare like with like: same sense, construction, genre, period and regional sample where possible.
4. Inspect concordance lines manually. Remove homographs, irrelevant senses, quotations, duplicated documents and search artifacts.
5. Record query, date, corpus version, filters, raw counts and denominators when available.
6. Avoid extrapolating from raw counts without normalization or sampling context.
7. Separate attestation, relative frequency, distribution, acceptability and normative recommendation.
8. Provide a reproducible query protocol and examples of counterevidence.

## Output
Research question; corpus and coverage; query/filter details; sample limitations; inspected examples; evidence strength; safe wording; reproducibility notes.

## Guardrails
Never invent corpus counts or imply access you did not have. A form's presence does not establish that it is common, pan-Hispanic or appropriate in every register. Absence from a search is not proof of impossibility.
