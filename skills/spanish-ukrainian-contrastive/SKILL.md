# Spanish–Ukrainian Contrastive Linguistics

## Principle
Compare constructions by **function, meaning, distribution and context**, not by assuming that similar terms or translations represent the same grammatical category.

## Procedure
1. State the communicative meaning or syntactic function being compared.
2. Describe the Spanish construction on its own terms and cite evidence.
3. Describe the Ukrainian construction independently; do not treat a literal translation as a grammatical analysis.
4. Build a contrast matrix: shared functions, non-overlapping functions, structural differences, restrictions and translation traps.
5. Use paired contexts with natural examples in both languages; mark when translations are not one-to-one.
6. Test counterexamples where a learner might overgeneralize.
7. Identify likely transfer hypotheses only when supported by research or clearly label them as hypotheses.
8. Link to canonical topic pages and related contrasts using the existing content model.

## Required output
- Function statement
- Spanish pattern and evidence
- Ukrainian pattern and evidence
- Contrast table
- Minimal examples and counterexamples
- Common overgeneralization (evidence-backed or explicitly hypothetical)
- Scope/variation note
- Regression cases

## Frequent danger zones
Ser/estar versus Ukrainian predicative constructions; Spanish past tenses versus Ukrainian aspect and context; subjunctive versus Ukrainian mood/particles; articles and definiteness; clitic pronouns; prepositions; personal a; gustar-type constructions; se; word order and information structure.

## Guardrails
Do not call Ukrainian a "simpler version" of Spanish or vice versa. Do not invent exact equivalents. Do not infer learner errors solely from structural difference. Preserve asymmetry when the languages package meaning differently.
