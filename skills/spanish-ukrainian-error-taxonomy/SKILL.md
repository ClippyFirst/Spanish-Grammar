# Spanish–Ukrainian Learner Error Taxonomy

## Purpose

Help the site explain likely difficulty points for Ukrainian-speaking learners through contrastive analysis and documented learner evidence. This is a hypothesis-management skill, not permission to invent learner statistics.

## Workflow

1. Define the target learner group and task: comprehension, controlled grammar, writing, speaking, translation or editing.
2. Extract candidate error patterns from actual learner data where available, published studies, teaching observations with provenance, or careful contrastive analysis.
3. Label each item by evidence class:
   - **Observed:** attested in a cited learner corpus, study or documented sample.
   - **Repeatedly reported:** supported by multiple relevant teaching/research sources.
   - **Contrastive hypothesis:** plausible from structural differences but not yet empirically verified.
   - **Anecdotal:** a single observation; never generalize to all learners.
4. Specify the exact Spanish construction, intended meaning, learner output, corrected form and why the error may arise.
5. Compare Spanish and Ukrainian by function and context, not by assuming one-to-one equivalence between labels.
6. Identify likely causes cautiously: transfer, overgeneralization, instruction-induced rule, processing load, lexical uncertainty or performance slip.
7. Design a minimal pair or diagnostic item that distinguishes the proposed cause from competing explanations.
8. Recommend teaching responses: contrastive explanation, retrieval practice, feedback, spaced review or prerequisite review.
9. Track evidence gaps and update the taxonomy when new data contradicts a hypothesis.

## Output

Pattern ID; Spanish target; observed/candidate error; intended meaning; evidence class; citations/data provenance; competing explanations; diagnostic item; teaching intervention; confidence and limitations.

## Guardrails

- Never claim “Ukrainian learners commonly make this error” without relevant evidence.
- Do not infer an individual learner's competence from one mistake.
- Do not equate Ukrainian and Spanish grammatical categories solely by translation.
- Avoid stereotypes and deficit framing; explain task and context.
- Do not publish learner data that identifies people without permission.
