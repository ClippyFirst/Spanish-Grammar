# Spanish Variation & Usage Auditor

## Scope dimensions
For every variable claim, identify relevant geography, register, genre, formality, speaker relationship and whether the variation is grammatical, lexical, pragmatic or orthographic. The repository-wide audit must cover a general/pan-Hispanic baseline where justified, Spain, Latin America as a macro-region, relevant individual countries and subregions, and usage presented as neutral/unmarked. Do not imply that 'neutral Spanish' is one uniform dialect or that 'Latin American Spanish' is homogeneous.

## Procedure
1. Highlight universal words such as "always", "never", "all Spanish speakers" and "incorrect".
2. Determine whether the page describes a pan-Hispanic pattern, a specific standard, a national/regional pattern, a common tendency, a register or colloquial usage. Keep normative status, frequency and neutrality separate.
3. Verify the claim with RAE/ASALE resources and appropriate regional/descriptive evidence; when a country-level claim is made, seek evidence for that country or state that the country-level coverage remains unverified.
4. Separate normative status from frequency, prestige, formality and local preference.
5. For voseo, address relevant pronoun and verb paradigms and distinguish geographic patterns; do not treat voseo as one uniform system.
6. For leísmo/laísmo/loísmo, clitic use, past-tense preferences and subjunctive variation, define the specific construction and region.
7. Propose a label and note that preserves the main explanation while making the scope explicit.

## Output
Claim; current implied scope; evidence; variation dimensions; recommended label; revised wording; examples to test; countries/regions actually checked; unverified regions; confidence and source gaps.

## Guardrails

- Build coverage across Spain, the Latin American macro-regions and individual countries when relevant; never treat the region list as proof that every country was checked.
- Keep a repository-level coverage matrix with statuses such as checked, relevant but not checked, not relevant to this claim, and evidence unavailable.
- Label 'general', 'pan-Hispanic', 'neutral' and 'unmarked' only with a defined meaning and evidence; do not use them as automatic synonyms.
Do not reduce Latin America to one variety. Do not mark a valid form as an error because it differs from a textbook default. Do not call every colloquial form non-standard; cite evidence. Avoid unsupported claims about social class or speaker identity.
